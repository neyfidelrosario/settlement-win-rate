# Findings: Which Discounts Close Deals Without Wasting Margin

## What I looked at

60 settlement offers. 46 closed. That's a 76.7% win rate at an average 24% discount, closing in under 20 days.

## The numbers by discount band

| Discount band | Offers | Win rate | Avg days to decision | Margin per win |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5–10% | 10 | 40.0% | 31.8 | $7,581.24 |
| 10–20% | 13 | 69.2% | 23.5 | $22,203.67 |
| 20–30% | 14 | 100.0% | 18.1 | $22,870.70 |
| 30%+ | 23 | 82.6% | 13.1 | $55,935.24 |

## The sweet spot is 20–30%. It's not close.

Every offer in the 20–30% band closed. 18 days average. $22,871 per win. Full win rate at a price I can defend.

## The 30%+ band is where margin goes to die.

Lower win rate than the band below it (82.6% vs 100%). Five days faster. Two and a half times the cost per win at $55,935. Over a million dollars in discounts given away in that band alone. Run the math: at the 20–30% price, those 19 wins cost about $435k. We paid $1.06M. That's roughly $628k of margin I'll never get back.

## Under 10% doesn't work either.

30% counter rate. Clients don't take it seriously, they push back. You don't save margin with a low opener. You negotiate twice.

## What I'd do

Cap first offers at 20–25%. Go above 30% only after two dead rounds or genuine financial distress, never as the default. Stop opening under 10%.

## What the numbers can't tell me

The toughest accounts get the biggest discounts, so part of that 30%+ miss rate is harder clients, not bad discounting. The data says we're over-discounting. It can't prove every big discount was wrong. The follow-up worth doing: track escalations and check whether the bigger number actually caused the yes.
