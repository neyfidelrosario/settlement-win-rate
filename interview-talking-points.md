# Interview Talking Points: Settlement Win-Rate Analysis

Plain-English points for talking about the project in interviews. Each point names the project step, then the business-analysis skill it proves. The dataset is fictional and representative; the workflow is real. Say so if asked.

## 1. "I started with the tradeoff, not the data."
**Project step:** Before touching data, I wrote down the real tension: every settlement is a tradeoff between winning the deal and keeping margin. A discount that wins but gives away too much is a loss dressed as a win.
**Skill this proves:** Framing the problem in business terms. The analysis exists to serve a decision, not the other way around.

## 2. "I compared win rate, speed, and margin cost together."
**Project step:** I grouped 60 offers into discount bands and measured three things per band: what share got accepted, how fast clients decided, and how many margin dollars each win cost.
**Skill this proves:** Multi-dimensional thinking. Looking at win rate alone says "bigger discounts win more." Adding margin cost flips the conclusion.

## 3. "I found the sweet spot — and the waste."
**Project step:** The 20–30% band won every offer at a reasonable margin cost. The 30%+ band actually won less often, closed only slightly faster, and cost more than double the margin per win. That top band is mostly waste.
**Skill this proves:** Turning analysis into a recommendation. The deliverable wasn't a chart — it was "cap first offers here, escalate only after this long."

## 4. "I did it in SQL."
**Project step:** Four queries: the big picture, win rate and speed by band, margin cost per win by band, and counter rates. Each one answers a specific question a manager would actually ask.
**Skill this proves:** Technical execution on a real workflow, not a textbook exercise.

## 5. "I was honest about what the data can't prove."
**Project step:** I flagged selection bias: the hardest accounts tend to get the biggest discounts, so the top band's weaker win rate partly reflects tougher clients, not just wasted discount. The data suggests waste; it doesn't prove every big discount was a mistake.
**Skill this proves:** Analytical maturity. Knowing the limits of your conclusion is what makes people trust your recommendations.

## 6. "This is how I actually negotiate."
**Project step:** The project mirrors my day-to-day judgment: open at a level that wins without overspending, escalate deliberately, and watch for the point where more discount stops buying more yeses.
**Skill this proves:** Domain expertise made visible. The analysis doesn't replace negotiation skill — it documents it.

## If they ask "Is this real data?"
"The figures and names are fictional and representative. The workflow — deciding what discount to offer and learning from what worked — is exactly what I do every day."
